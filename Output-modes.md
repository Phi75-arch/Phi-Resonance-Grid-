


---

Output Modes — Speaking the Same Signal in Different Voices

Φ-Resonance Grid separates signal from voice.

The underlying analysis remains the same, but how findings are expressed can vary to match different cognitive and cultural preferences.


---

Why Output Modes Matter

Developers respond differently to feedback. A single tone can:

Increase resistance

Trigger defensiveness

Reduce adoption


Providing multiple voices allows the same insight to reach more people without changing its meaning.


---

Available Output Modes

🎵 Poetic (Default)

Tone: Reflective, metaphor-rich, invitational

Example:

> “This function carries many branches. Consider whether a gentler flow might emerge through separation.”



Best for:

Exploratory use

Research contexts

Developers receptive to metaphor



---

⚖️ Neutral

Tone: Calm, descriptive, minimal metaphor

Example:

> “Function complexity is 9. Suggested target: ≤ 6. Refactoring may improve readability.”



Best for:

Academic evaluation

Mixed teams

Documentation and reports



---

🧱 Strict

Tone: Direct, concise, low emotional framing

Example:

> “Complexity exceeds heuristic threshold. Consider refactoring.”



Best for:

Users who prefer traditional tooling

Environments sensitive to tone



---

Design Constraint

Output modes:

Must never change severity or findings

Must never introduce enforcement

Must never shame or moralize


They are translations, not transformations.


---

Extending Output Modes

New modes are welcome if they:

Preserve semantic meaning

Reduce friction for a specific audience

Do not reintroduce coercive language


Proposals should include before/after examples.


---

Closing Thought

> The same truth spoken differently can either close a mind or open it.



Output modes exist to keep minds open.
